Rainbow's Arc Positioning

Position from the sky when GPS is gone.

Your phone becomes a camera sextant. Photograph the sun and the sea horizon in one frame; the picture and the clock do the rest — a line of position in seconds, a latitude-and-longitude fix from two sights, and a deck log that keeps honest dead reckoning between them. Fully offline, a thousand miles from the nearest signal.

For iPhone · on the App Store

What it does

  • One photo is the sight Sun and horizon together in the frame. The horizon is the level reference, so the boat's roll and your grip don't matter — hold the phone any way that works.
  • The whole reduction, worked for you Dip, refraction, semidiameter, parallax — the same corrections a paper sight reduction works, shown line by line, against a built-in almanac good through 2050.
  • Marking you can mean Draggable handles with a magnifier loupe, then one-pixel nudge arrows — because on a sun sight, a pixel is roughly a nautical mile.
  • A real deck log Anchor a known position, log course-and-speed legs, tap Stopped when you heave to. Every sight reduces against the DR for its own moment; every fix can anchor a fresh reckoning.
  • Fixes that own their error Cross any two sights into a running fix with a stated radius — and see the gap between where the log said you'd be and where the sky says you are. That gap is the current talking.
  • Noon, predicted Local apparent noon computed for your longitude with a countdown. A sight near the meridian reads directly as latitude — the classic noon sight, kept classic.

A camera sight is typically good to a few nautical miles, and every result says exactly how sure it is. When a sight isn't trustworthy — sun too low, camera angle too uncertain, a line falling absurdly far from your reckoning — the app says so in plain language instead of pretending. This is a backup for when GPS is jammed, spoofed, or dead. It is not a chartplotter, and it never claims to be.

Never look directly at the sun. Every sight can be taken with the screen doing the looking — the app is built around that rule, and so is the manual.
